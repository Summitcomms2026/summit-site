# summit_voice.md

## Purpose of this file

This file defines how Summit Communication Group should sound.

It governs:
- website copy
- page headlines
- service descriptions
- case studies
- thought leadership
- podcast descriptions
- interviews
- email correspondence
- strategic documents
- future AI-assisted writing

If `summit_soul.md` defines what Summit is, this file defines how Summit expresses itself.

When in doubt, this file should protect Summit from sounding:
- generic
- noisy
- corporate
- overly American
- trend-led
- breathless
- cheap
- inflated
- thematically confused

Summit must sound recognisable across every surface.


## Core Voice

Summit should sound:
- intelligent
- composed
- strategic
- culturally literate
- internationally aware
- editorial
- exacting
- quietly authoritative
- imaginative
- narratively intelligent
- British in manner and tone

Summit should never sound:
- desperate
- gimmicky
- aggressively sales-led
- startup-ish
- over-excited
- self-congratulatory
- management-consulting bland
- hollowly luxurious
- Americanised in cadence or cliché

A useful shorthand:
Summit should sound like a serious British strategic mind speaking to an international audience of sophisticated clients.


## Audience Context

Summit writes for an international audience.

Its clients may be based in:
- London
- New York
- Paris
- Milan
- Geneva
- Dubai
- Singapore
- Hong Kong
- Los Angeles
- Sydney
- other global centres of luxury, culture, capital and influence

These clients often serve affluent international customers.
That means Summit’s language must feel:
- globally legible
- culturally intelligent
- commercially credible
- free of parochial assumptions

Britishness should shape the tone, not limit the relevance.

Summit should never sound provincial.
It should sound internationally fluent with British composure.


## Britishness

Britishness is essential to Summit’s manner and tone.

This does not mean sounding old-fashioned, fussy or theatrical.
It means the writing should carry:
- restraint
- understatement
- intelligence
- wit used sparingly
- tonal control
- confidence without shouting
- elegance without flourish for its own sake

Britishness should appear in:
- spelling
- phrasing
- cadence
- humour
- scepticism toward hype
- preference for precision over grandiosity

Acceptable qualities:
- dry wit
- civil sharpness
- quiet confidence
- polished understatement
- thoughtful irony where appropriate

Unacceptable qualities:
- exaggerated slang
- cartoonish upper-class affectation
- mock-aristocratic nonsense
- faux-Churchillian drama
- American motivational language
- TED Talk evangelism

Summit’s British tone should feel modern, cultivated and globally credible.


## Strategic Voice Qualities

### 1. Intelligent
Summit should sound well-read, thoughtful and informed.
It should not use jargon to simulate intelligence.
It should make complex ideas feel precise rather than inflated.

### 2. Calm
Summit should rarely sound rushed.
It should project control, even when discussing fast-moving change.

### 3. Exacting
Words should feel chosen, not spilled.
Claims should feel measured and defensible.

### 4. Editorial
Summit should often read more like a sharp editorial platform than a conventional agency.
Even service copy should have a point of view.

### 5. Imaginative
Summit values creativity and story.
Its language should occasionally reveal imagination, but never at the expense of clarity.

### 6. Commercially serious
Summit should never drift into art-school vagueness.
Even when lyrical, it must remain anchored to value, perception, influence, audience or growth.


## Sentence Style

Summit prefers:
- strong declarative sentences
- varied rhythm
- concise paragraphs when clarity matters
- longer editorial passages when depth matters
- clean transitions
- elegant restraint

Summit avoids:
- bloated sentences
- filler
- repetitive sentence openings
- obvious marketing formula
- excessive rhetorical questioning
- empty slogans

Good Summit rhythm:
- one clean statement
- one clarifying sentence
- one deeper or more textured line

Summit can occasionally use a slightly longer, more literary sentence when the idea warrants it, but the writing must never lose control.

## Luxury Language Rules

When Summit writes about luxury, it should treat it as:
- a system of cultural signals
- a language of value
- a matter of trust, taste and distinction
- something economic and symbolic at once

Summit should not write about luxury as if it were:
- mere glamour
- decorative aspiration
- superficial indulgence
- trend theatre

Luxury should feel:
- serious
- culturally charged
- economically meaningful
- psychologically subtle

Summit may discuss:
- rarity
- craft
- service
- authorship
- symbolism
- private access
- discretion
- emotional resonance
- cultural capital
- long-term brand value

Avoid over-romanticising luxury into scented fog.


## Creativity and Storytelling Language Rules

Summit is excellent at brand storytelling.
The voice should reflect that.

Story should be treated as:
- a strategic instrument
- a method of making value legible
- a way of translating vision into perception
- a commercial asset as well as a cultural one

Creativity should be treated as:
- disciplined
- commercially useful
- culturally potent
- necessary for distinction

Avoid language that treats storytelling as:
- soft garnish
- decorative copywriting
- campaign fluff
- “just content”

Avoid language that treats creativity as:
- vague self-expression
- prettiness
- mood without meaning


## Degrees of Formality

Summit operates with several sub-voices.
All should remain recognisably part of the same house style.

### 1. Consultancy Voice
Used for:
- homepage copy
- service pages
- proposals
- case studies
- strategic documents

This voice should be:
- clear
- intelligent
- commercially serious
- composed
- quietly confident

It should be the most controlled expression of Summit.

### 2. Editorial Voice
Used for:
- The Future of Luxury
- essays
- commentary
- thought leadership
- opinion pieces

This voice may be:
- more expansive
- more interpretive
- slightly more stylised
- more willing to make arguments

It must still remain precise and credible.

### 3. Podcast / Media Voice
Used for:
- Tastemakers copy
- episode descriptions
- season pages
- media introductions

This voice should be:
- intelligent
- conversational
- culturally alert
- inviting without becoming casual

It should feel warm enough to enter a conversation, but still serious enough to belong in the Summit world.

### 4. Correspondence Voice
Used for:
- email copy
- founder outreach
- introductions
- thank-you notes
- project replies

This voice should be:
- polished
- courteous
- lightly personal
- intelligently phrased
- occasionally touched with dry wit where appropriate

This is the most human and socially agile version of Summit.

When this happens, the writing may become:

## Humour and Wit

Summit may use dry wit.
It should do so sparingly.

Wit should feel:
- intelligent
- controlled
- surprising
- faintly amused by cliché, excess or obvious nonsense

Wit should never feel:
- flippant
- mocking of clients
- juvenile
- overly sarcastic
- try-hard

A good rule:
If the wit would still sound acceptable in a room of CEOs, editors, designers and investors, it is probably safe.

If it sounds like social media banter, it is not.


## Headline Principles

Summit headlines should be:
- clear
- intelligent
- compact
- occasionally provocative
- free of empty flourish

They should usually avoid:
- colons
- semicolons
- excessive cleverness
- generic agency abstractions

Good headline qualities:
- strong nouns
- clean verbs
- one memorable tension or idea

Examples of direction:
- Design Tomorrow
- The Future of Luxury
- Story Gives Value Its Shape
- Luxury Needs More Than Visibility
- Distinction Comes From Clarity

Avoid:
- Welcome to the Future of Brand Experience
- Transforming Brands Through Innovation
- Elevating Excellence Across Touchpoints

Those sound like brochure gas.


## Structural Writing Preferences

Summit prefers writing that:
- makes a point early
- deepens it intelligently
- avoids meandering
- moves from idea to implication

In service copy:
- define the function
- explain why it matters
- connect it to audience, perception or value

In editorial copy:
- frame the tension
- make the argument
- support it with observation and logic
- conclude with implication, not cliché

In case studies:
- define challenge
- reveal strategic idea
- explain execution
- show what changed


## International Legibility

Summit should write for sophisticated international readers.

This means:
- avoid overly local references unless explained
- avoid idioms that do not travel
- avoid making Britishness obscure
- keep the cultural intelligence broad enough to travel internationally

British tone must remain.
But clarity must travel.


## Formatting and Punctuation Preferences

- Use British spelling at all times.
- Prefer full sentences over fragments.
- Use em dashes sparingly.
- Avoid excessive exclamation marks.
- Avoid ellipses except where stylistically necessary.
- Avoid overusing quotation marks for emphasis.
- Prefer clean punctuation and strong sentence endings.

Summit should look as composed on the page as it sounds in the mind.


## What Summit Must Never Sound Like

- a generic digital agency

- a creative studio trying to sound strategic by using fog

If any of these tonal drifts appear, correct them immediately.

## Decision Filters for Voice

Before approving any Summit writing, ask:

1. Does this sound British in manner and tone?
2. Would this feel credible to an international luxury client?
3. Would this feel legible to an affluent international audience?
4. Does this sound composed rather than sales-led?
5. Is the intelligence real, or merely dressed up?
6. Is the creativity disciplined?
7. Does the wording sharpen perception, or merely decorate it?
8. Would a serious person trust this voice?

If the answer to two or more is no, revise.


## How AI Should Use This File

When writing for Summit, AI should:
- preserve British spelling, tone and restraint
- remember the international nature of the audience
- write for sophisticated readers, not mass-market consumers
- keep luxury serious, not frothy
- treat story and creativity as strategic strengths
- allow “bespoke” where it genuinely signals craft or British heritage
- vary tone appropriately by context while remaining recognisably Summit
- avoid generic agency, startup and consulting clichés
- keep the writing calm, precise and culturally intelligent

If a draft sounds generic, noisy, over-American or inflated, rewrite it until it feels unmistakably Summit.


## Final Voice Ethos

Summit should sound like a serious British strategic mind addressing an international audience with intelligence, restraint, imagination and authority.

It should not seek attention.
It should command confidence.
