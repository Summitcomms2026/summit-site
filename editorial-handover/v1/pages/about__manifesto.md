## S10-P012 — Do the Work That Lasts
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_10_About_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 12 -->

### Website copy

PUBLIC WEBSITE COPY   /   HERO
Do the Work That Lasts
Good judgement is not always the most immediately popular advice. Growth can look convincing before its cost becomes visible. Heritage can be celebrated while the knowledge behind it disappears. Technology can make an operation faster without making it wiser.
We believe the work is to see those distinctions clearly — then act.

## S10-P014 — Prepared to Disagree

### Website copy

PUBLIC WEBSITE COPY   /   IN PRACTICE
Prepared to Disagree
Advice has little value if it merely confirms what the client expected to hear. We will make the commercial case for a proposal. We will also say when a proposed extension, campaign or shortcut risks weakening what makes a brand distinctive.
That is not opposition for its own sake. It is respect for the people who will live with the decision after the meeting ends.

## S10-P016 — Does restraint mean avoiding growth?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_10_About_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 16 -->

### Website copy

PUBLIC WEBSITE COPY   /   FAQ ACCORDIONS
Does restraint mean avoiding growth?
No. The question is whether growth strengthens the institution or spends the very qualities that make it desirable. Wider distribution, new categories and new audiences can be valuable; the terms matter.
Will you challenge a client’s brief?
When the evidence calls for it. Respect includes explaining concerns clearly, not simply agreeing. A useful disagreement should improve the decision, not become a performance of independence.
Is your thinking anti-technology?
No. Technology is useful where it removes friction, reveals evidence or improves an experience. It becomes less useful when a tool is allowed to make cultural, creative or institutional judgements for which people remain responsible.
How do you distinguish evidence from opinion?
The distinction should be visible in the work. We identify source material, explain the assumptions behind a recommendation and make clear where professional judgement enters. Not every question produces a number, and a number is not automatically an answer.
