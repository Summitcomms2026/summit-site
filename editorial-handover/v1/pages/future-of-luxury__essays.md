## S09-P013 — Can I search for a particular maison?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_09_Future_of_Luxury_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 13 -->

### Website copy

Can I search for a particular maison?
Yes. A maison is an indexable subject, distinct from the essay’s primary argument. Searching Hermès should bring together ownership, iconic products, governance, scarcity and other relevant essays rather than one narrowly labelled category.

### Photography, layout and interaction notes

Maintain entity aliases and accents in search.

## S09-P014 — Why separate themes from sectors?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_09_Future_of_Luxury_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 14 -->

### Website copy

Why separate themes from sectors?
A sector tells you where an essay takes place. A theme tells you what it is about. An automotive essay on volume strategy may be just as relevant to a fashion executive considering distribution.

### Photography, layout and interaction notes

Useful cross-sector recommendation rationale.

## S09-P015 — Are older essays rewritten when circumstances change?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_09_Future_of_Luxury_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 15 -->

### Website copy

Are older essays rewritten when circumstances change?
The original argument and publication date should remain clear. If a material development changes the context, add a dated editor’s note or a link to a later essay; do not silently rewrite historical analysis.

### Photography, layout and interaction notes

Distinct datePublished and dateModified metadata.

## S09-P016 — What if a search finds nothing?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_09_Future_of_Luxury_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 16 -->

### Website copy

What if a search finds nothing?
The archive should say so plainly, offer broader themes and allow the reader to clear filters. Missing material is a reason to improve indexing, not to invent a result.

### Photography, layout and interaction notes

Clear empty state; no dead end.
