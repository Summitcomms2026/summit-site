Automation With Judgement
Not every task deserves an agent. Some should remain firmly in human hands.
Agentic AI explores where systems can carry out defined sequences of work — retrieving, checking, preparing or routing information — with permissions, records and human approval appropriate to the risk.
The question is not what can be automated. It is what should be.

### Website copy

What Must Be Agreed First
Scope
A bounded job and a practical measure of success.
Information
Which records can be accessed, what must remain private and how sources are checked.
Authority
Which actions are allowed, which require approval and how exceptions are handled.
Accountability
Who owns the output, monitors performance and can stop the system.
If those questions cannot be answered, the work is not ready for deployment.

## S07-P042 — What makes this different from ordinary AI software?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_07_Programmes_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 42 -->

### Website copy

PUBLIC-FACING COPY
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Good Questions
What makes this different from ordinary AI software?
An agent is designed to undertake a sequence of permitted actions in pursuit of a specific task, rather than only generating an answer to a single prompt. Whether that is useful depends on workflow design, data access, permissions and the human controls around it.
Would an agent speak to our private clients?
Only if a defined use case, consent arrangements, identity disclosure, quality controls and escalation route justify that design. For highly personal client relationships, preparing information for a human adviser may be a much better application than autonomous contact.

## S07-P043 — Can we use confidential archives or customer information?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_07_Programmes_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 43 -->

### Website copy

PUBLIC-FACING COPY
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Good Questions
Can we use confidential archives or customer information?
Possibly, but not by default. Source rights, confidentiality, privacy requirements, vendor terms, retention, access control and secure deployment must be assessed before any sensitive material is connected. The first conversation should not include confidential files.
How do we know when not to automate?
Begin with the consequences of a mistake. Tasks requiring cultural judgement, legal interpretation, an expression of personal trust or irreversible action often need a human decision. Automation should reduce unnecessary work while preserving responsibility where it belongs.
