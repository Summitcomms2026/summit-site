## S07-P018 — Does thought leadership need a controversial opinion?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_07_Programmes_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 18 -->

### Website copy

PUBLIC-FACING COPY
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Good Questions
Does thought leadership need a controversial opinion?
No. It needs a question the organisation can answer with knowledge that matters to someone else. A measured argument, a useful piece of evidence or a carefully made distinction can be more durable than provocation.
Who writes the material?
That depends on the engagement. Summit can develop an editorial framework, commission or draft material and work closely with the leader or specialist whose name and knowledge it carries. Authorship, approvals and any permitted use of AI should be agreed before publication.

## S07-P019 — Can you guarantee press coverage or invitations?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_07_Programmes_Copy_FAQs_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 19 -->

### Website copy

PUBLIC-FACING COPY
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Good Questions
Can you guarantee press coverage or invitations?
No credible editorial practice should. We can identify relevant publications, editors, conversations and channels, then prepare material that merits attention. Editorial decisions remain with the people and institutions making them.
What if our organisation has sensitive or disputed facts?
Those are reasons to slow the process, not hide the difficulty in polished copy. We establish what can be supported, what requires legal or specialist review and what should not yet be published. A strong reputation is rarely improved by an avoidable correction.
