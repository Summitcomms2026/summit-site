## S13-P093 — Protect the Substance
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_13_Industry_Function_Copy_and_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 93 -->

### Website copy

PROTECT THE SUBSTANCE
Protect the Substance
Some assets are protected by registration; others depend on contractual discipline, secrecy, authorship records or continued institutional use. An archive may be rich in material but poor in usable rights. A celebrated icon may dominate the house without a clear plan for renewal. We work to make those distinctions legible, connecting the creative proposition to the rights and governance needed to carry it. The work is not merely a retrospective exercise: protection decisions should shape new products and collaborations before they become public.

## S13-P097 — Questions We Hear
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_13_Industry_Function_Copy_and_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 97 -->

### Website copy

FAQs
Questions We Hear
Can Summit register trademarks or provide legal advice?
Legal work belongs to qualified counsel. Summit’s role is to connect product, brand, patrimony and commercial questions, working with appropriate legal specialists where commissioned.
What if the archive is fragmented?
Start by identifying what exists, what can be substantiated and what can lawfully be used. An incomplete archive should be described honestly, not filled with convenient mythology.
Can an icon be protected through communications alone?
No. Communications can help sustain a coherent narrative, but product decisions, rights, production discipline and governance determine what the house can actually protect.
