WHERE HAS THE DESIRE GONE?
Where Has the Desire Gone?
Global visibility can support a category while eroding an individual product’s distance from the ordinary. We examine signature products, range extension, packaging, claims, channel pricing, retail education and the relationship between repeat purchase and prestige. In fragrance, mythology may be carried by the maison long after the object has become familiar. In skincare, scientific credibility and the legitimacy of the experience have to remain distinct from promising more than evidence supports.

## S13-P020 — Questions We Hear
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_13_Industry_Function_Copy_and_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 20 -->

### Website copy

FAQs
Questions We Hear
Can a widely available fragrance remain luxury?
It can retain cultural standing, but availability, pricing and narrative governance affect what the individual product continues to signify. The maison and its fragrance should not be assessed as if they were the same object.
Should every hero product have a wider range?
Only where extension serves the institution and customer without erasing the reason the original mattered. Greater SKU count is not proof of stronger luxury standing.
Can digital performance and exclusivity coexist?
Yes, when the path to discovery and purchase is consistent with the desired relationship. Conversion need not mean blanket discounts or indiscriminate distribution.
