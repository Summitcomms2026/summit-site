WHAT SHOULD STAY RECOGNISABLE?
What Should Stay Recognisable?
Creative leadership changes. Distribution expands. Campaigns accelerate and categories multiply. Each decision can be reasonable alone while leaving the maison less distinctive in aggregate. We look at signatures, product families, licensing history, price and channel discipline, archive use and the balance between cultural attention and recognisable institutional identity. A heritage reference should not be repeated because it is available. Nor should it be abandoned because a new season needs a story.

## S13-P013 — Questions We Hear
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_13_Industry_Function_Copy_and_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 13 -->

### Website copy

FAQs
Questions We Hear
Does repositioning require a new identity?
Not necessarily. Often the work is to decide what the house must preserve, then adjust how its offer, experience or communications express it.
How should a maison use its archive?
As working material for present decisions, not a retrospective display. Archive activation should help the institution create and protect new meaning.
When does collaboration become dilution?
When the association earns short-lived reach at the expense of a coherent product, distribution or institutional story. The answer depends on the partners and terms, not a blanket ban.
