## S13-P023 — What Cannot Be Rushed?
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_13_Industry_Function_Copy_and_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 23 -->

### Website copy

WHAT CANNOT BE RUSHED?
What Cannot Be Rushed?
A château, distillery or grower works with material constraints that marketing cannot cancel. Release policy, allocation, provenance, packaging, hospitality and relationships with collectors all shape how an object enters culture. Scarcity can arise from land and vintage; it can also be exercised through deliberate restraint. Those are different claims. We consider the difference between a story rooted in production and one assembled after the fact.

## S13-P027 — Questions We Hear
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_13_Industry_Function_Copy_and_Cliff_Notes_v01.docx table 27 -->

### Website copy

FAQs
Questions We Hear
Can a house create scarcity through marketing?
Marketing can describe a genuine production or allocation discipline; it cannot make a manufactured shortage equivalent to an enduring philosophy of restraint.
Why does provenance matter to brand strategy?
Provenance is part of the substance being offered. Where origin, storage, ownership or release history matters, the house needs a coherent way to substantiate its claims.
How should hospitality connect to the brand?
It should make the house’s standards evident in a real encounter, not simply provide an expensive setting for the logo.
