## S04-P008 — Every Encounter Counts
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_04_Patrimony_Experience_Thought_Leadership_v01.docx table 8 -->

### Website copy

CLIENT EXPERIENCE
Every Encounter Counts
An excellent boutique cannot rescue an indifferent digital introduction. Nor does an elaborate purchase ritual compensate for silence after the sale. The experience has to hold together.
Before the Purchase
Discovery · First contact · Narrative entry · Object encounter
In the Relationship
Service ritual · Private access · Continuity of care
Over Time
Aftercare · Archive return · Advocacy
We look for what is inconsistent, unnecessary or missing — then decide where better judgement would make the greatest difference.

### Photography, layout and interaction notes

SOURCE  Adapted editorial grouping of the eight-stage Luxury Experience Stack in The New Asian Maison, pp. 35–36. Full source stages remain documented for programme work.
LAYOUT  Three calm columns with a connecting rule, not numbered boxes or a funnel diagram.
IMAGE  Optional sequence of three owned photographs showing digital, object and aftercare. One coherent shoot is better.
INTERACTION  Columns can expose a short note on click; should remain readable without Javascript.
CONVERSION  No CTA inside each stage. Follow with a single question-led selector.

## S04-P009 — Where Does It Break?

### Website copy

CLIENT EXPERIENCE
Where Does It Break?
Choose the point where the experience feels least like the maison.
First contact
The right clients arrive, but the introduction does not earn their attention.
In the room
The product is excellent. The encounter around it is not.
After the purchase
The relationship becomes quiet at precisely the wrong moment.
Returning clients
Loyal clients receive transactions when they deserve recognition.
Discuss the Experience  →

### Photography, layout and interaction notes

INTERACTION  Four-choice experience selector. Display a tailored follow-up question and one relevant cross-link, not a fake assessment.
FORMAT  Two by two unnumbered choice cards on desktop; compact single-column buttons on mobile.
CONVERSATION  Pass selected stage into enquiry context. Always allow visitor to edit what is sent.
ANALYTICS  Only aggregate click events, no response-level tracking without consent.
CAUTION  Do not introduce a “luxury experience index” or assert proprietary scoring.

## S04-P010 — Design the Relationship, Not Just the Room
<!-- source: Summit_Website_Pass_04_Patrimony_Experience_Thought_Leadership_v01.docx table 10 -->

### Website copy

HOW WE WORK
Design the Relationship, Not Just the Room
Work begins with the people, the objects and the promises the maison already makes. We review the existing experience, agree what should remain unmistakably its own and develop the changes that matter most.
That may involve client journeys, service language, digital touchpoints, appointment rituals, private events, aftercare or the systems supporting them.
When a brief calls for specialist experience and innovation work, we assemble the appropriate collaborators. The client engages Summit; the practice is shaped around the brief.

### Photography, layout and interaction notes

IMAGE  Show a substantive design artefact — approved customer-journey mapping, contact sheet, user research evidence or an original service detail.
LAYOUT  Text-led two columns. Do not publish a fictional customer journey chart as if client evidence.
SPECIALISTS  The New Asian Maison names Cecilia Ercoli as a potential specialist collaborator where appropriate. Never imply standing engagement.
CROSS-SELL  “How We Work” can connect to the About site section without using an extra button here.
