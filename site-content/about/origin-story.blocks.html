The evolution

Not a chronology of offices. The story of a question.

Summit did not set out to become a research practice, a publisher or an observatory. Each arrived because the previous form of the work could not answer what it had started to ask.

I Design

It began with the work.

Summit began in strategy, brand, experience and digital work for luxury: positions, identities, platforms and the encounters around them. The work was at its best when it answered a question larger than the brief.

II The question

The briefs kept asking something else.

Client work kept raising questions that ordinary brand strategy could not answer:

  • Why do some objects accumulate authority, while others accumulate only fame?
  • Why do some houses keep their meaning through changes of owner, designer and market?
  • How can a name be everywhere and still be institutionally weak?
  • What does governance have to do with desire?
  • What survives once the marketing stops?

III Touch Me Not

A book that would not settle for opinion.

Olivia Dhordain’s note proposed a more serious book on luxury, and ended on a question: could data and AI strip luxury of its mystery and reveal the formula? Answering it meant building an instrument able to contradict its own authors — and then publishing what it said.

IV The Luxury 100

The argument became a reading.

At the centre of the instrument is a governed annual reading of durable luxury objects: evidence frozen on a stated date, every position read again, and one chair left empty when no object has earned it.

The Luxury 100

V The Observatory

The method went into the field.

Beyond the Canon lies the field: houses, objects and institutions read under the same method, with the same disclosure of confidence and the same refusal to claim what the evidence cannot support.

The Observatory

VI The Future of Luxury

The judgement went public.

The Future of Luxury applies the same standard to current events — appointments, acquisitions, launches and decisions — in essays written for the people who have to make them.

The Future of Luxury

VII Summit now

Connected, and governed.

Client work, research, editorial practice and proprietary intelligence now inform one another. They are kept apart where they must be: coverage cannot be bought, research is published on its own terms, and what a client shares stays with the client.
