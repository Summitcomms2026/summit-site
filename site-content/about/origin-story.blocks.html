From Attention to Authority

Reputation, culture, digital experience and commercial growth were never separate problems in serious brand work. They meet in the decisions a business makes about its identity and its future.

The move deeper into luxury brought a more demanding question. When does a successful brand become an institution whose meaning survives its founders, fashions and management cycles?

That question changed the work without requiring us to abandon where it began.

An Argument We Could Test

A correspondence between Gregory Gray and intellectual-property specialist Olivia Dhordain became Touch Me Not: The Mystery of Luxury. Rather than repeat the industry’s familiar claims about aura, the authors built a framework capable of producing findings they might not like.

The Luxury 100 and The Observatory grew from that work: a way to examine objects, houses and institutions through evidence rather than admiration alone.

FAQs

Did Summit start as a luxury consultancy?

Summit’s earlier public work centred on communications, storytelling, brand development and trust. The present luxury focus builds on that experience. Our research has given the practice a more explicit way to examine lasting institutional value.

Did Touch Me Not establish Summit?

No. The book began as a separate intellectual inquiry between Gregory Gray and Olivia Dhordain. It became an important foundation for Summit’s current luxury research and advisory work, but it was not the origin of the company.

Why does a book matter to clients?

Because it shows how the thinking is tested. The project documented a method, exposed uncomfortable findings and acknowledged its limits. That discipline can improve the questions put to a business even when a published ranking is not relevant to the brief.

Does Summit still work beyond luxury?

The practice’s experience includes communications, brand, digital experience and corporate advisory. Its current positioning emphasises luxury and other high-value categories where reputation, culture and enduring value matter. Specific sector credentials should be confirmed rather than inferred.
