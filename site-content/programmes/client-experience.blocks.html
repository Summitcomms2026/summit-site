Begin With One Moment

Some briefs need a complete journey review. Others begin with a single point of friction: private appointment, first enquiry, product delivery or service after purchase.

Examining that moment can expose the organisational choices beneath it. The improvement should serve the client and the institution, not merely look better in a presentation.

Good Questions

Does this concern retail only?

No. The client experience includes digital discovery, editorial encounters, private appointments, hospitality, purchase and aftercare. For some businesses the defining moment is not a shop at all; it may be a service call, an investment conversation or the handling of a treasured object.

How do you improve experience without adding theatre?

By starting with what the maison promises and how people actually encounter it. A credible improvement may be a better explanation, a slower handover, a skilled adviser or a repair process that works. Spectacle is optional; institutional coherence is not.

Will this replace our existing service team or designers?

The work should involve the people already responsible for the experience. Summit can bring strategic and design expertise to a defined question, then help teams agree what must change. Specialist support is engaged where the scope warrants it.

How would we know whether it works?

Measures depend on the experience being improved. Repeated enquiries, completion rates, service resolution, relevant client feedback or repeat engagement can provide evidence. We would establish a baseline and avoid mistaking superficial satisfaction scores for the quality of a long-term client relationship.
