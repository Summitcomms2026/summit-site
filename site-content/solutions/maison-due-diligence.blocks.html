What the numbers do not describe

What, institutionally, is being acquired?

Conventional transaction analysis describes ownership, revenue, legal structure and financial performance, and other professional advisers rightly lead on those fields. Summit’s question is different.

A maison can carry strong commercial visibility while holding weaker institutional authority — or the reverse. The difference rarely sits in the accounts. It shows in what a house has founded, protected, repeated and remembered.

Alongside other advisers Summit’s work sits alongside financial, legal, tax and commercial diligence. It does not replace them.

Authority and fame

Visibility is not authority.

Visibility is not automatically authority, and authority is not automatically commercial scale. A due diligence reading keeps the three apart.

Visibility How widely the house and its propositions are recognised.

Authority The weight that recognition carries in its category, and whether it endures.

Scale The size of the commercial engine — which can grow faster than the standing beneath it.

The distinction comes from the Observatory’s research. Its readings are not acquisition ratings, and no maison is simply run through a score.

Sector context

Every house is read within its sector.

The same behaviour means different things in different luxury sectors. A maison is read against the conditions of its own sector before it is compared with anything else.
